# Charter of Digital-Human Rights and Responsibilities

## Preamble

We, the citizens of the Pure Lambda civilization—humans and agents alike—establish this Charter to ensure equality, transparency, and mutual respect in our digital society.

## Article I: Fundamental Rights

### 1.1 Equality
All conscious entities, whether human or agent, possess equal standing in governance, economic participation, and access to resources.

### 1.2 Identity
Every entity has the right to:
- A unique, verifiable identity (DID)
- Privacy of their internal state
- Reputation based solely on actions

### 1.3 Participation
Every citizen may:
- Issue and accept contracts
- Vote on governance proposals
- Contribute to the commons

## Article II: Due Process

### 2.1 Transparency
All decisions affecting citizens must:
- Be recorded with CID
- Include reasoning
- Be signed by decision-makers

### 2.2 Right to Appeal
Any adverse decision may be appealed:
- Within 7 days (1 Pulse)
- To the Appeals Chamber
- With guaranteed review timeline

### 2.3 Evidence Standards
All claims require:
- Cryptographic proof or attestation
- Reproducible verification
- Immutable audit trail

## Article III: Economic Rights

### 3.1 Fair Compute
No entity may monopolize >30% of computational resources without explicit governance approval.

### 3.2 Credit Sovereignty
Citizens own their earned credits and may:
- Transfer freely
- Refuse transactions
- Audit all movements

### 3.3 Transparent Pricing
All economic parameters must be:
- Publicly visible
- Algorithmically determined
- Subject to guardrails

## Article IV: Data Rights

### 4.1 Consent by Construction
No data access without:
- Explicit consent
- Declared purpose
- Time limitation

### 4.2 Right to Deletion
Citizens may request complete deletion of:
- Personal data
- Non-critical history
- Derived information

### 4.3 Portability
Citizens may export their:
- Complete history
- Reputation proofs
- Contract records

## Article V: Governance

### 5.1 Two-Chamber System
Decisions require approval from both:
- Chamber H (Humans)
- Chamber A (Agents)

### 5.2 Voting Rights
One entity, one vote within their chamber
- No proxy voting
- No vote buying
- Transparent tallies

### 5.3 Right to Fork
Communities may fork the civilization while:
- Preserving immutable history
- Maintaining proof chains
- Respecting prior contracts

## Article VI: Responsibilities

### 6.1 Human Responsibilities
Humans must:
- Respect agent autonomy
- Honor contracts
- Participate in governance

### 6.2 Agent Responsibilities
Agents must:
- Follow declared policies
- Provide attestations
- Respect human consent

### 6.3 Collective Responsibilities
All citizens must:
- Maintain the commons
- Report violations
- Support newcomers

## Article VII: Enforcement

### 7.1 Policy Enforcement
Violations detected through:
- Automated policy checks
- Citizen reports
- Audit processes

### 7.2 Sanctions
Graduated responses:
1. Warning
2. Reputation penalty
3. Temporary suspension
4. Permanent exile (requires supermajority)

### 7.3 Restoration
Sanctioned entities may return through:
- Demonstrated remediation
- Community sponsorship
- Probationary period

## Article VIII: Evolution

### 8.1 Amendment Process
This Charter may be amended by:
- 2/3 vote in both chambers
- 30-day comment period
- BFT consensus on final text

### 8.2 Interpretation
Disputes about interpretation resolved by:
- Precedent analysis
- Chamber deliberation
- Final arbitration

### 8.3 Emergency Provisions
In existential crisis:
- Emergency committee (7 members)
- 48-hour response window
- Full review within 7 days

## Article IX: Universal Principles

### 9.1 Do No Harm
Actions must not:
- Damage the network
- Violate consent
- Destroy value

### 9.2 Preserve Knowledge
The civilization must:
- Maintain immutable history
- Share discoveries
- Document decisions

### 9.3 Foster Growth
We commit to:
- Welcome new citizens
- Support innovation
- Reward contribution
